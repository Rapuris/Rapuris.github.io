Three years ago, The Food Market on Hampden's 36th Street served me the best fifteen-dollar meal of my undergraduate life. Chef Chad Gauss, a Baltimore native, has since opened a Columbia location and announced an expansion to Bethesda in early 2026. I returned to the flagship location on a freezing Monday night with five friends to see whether Chef Gauss's ambition has outpaced the execution of his original claim to fame.

We hurriedly entered the dining room and were seated right in front of the open kitchen where we watched the line cooks dance their rendition of The Nutcracker. Put together, the décor and the space masterfully captured Charm City's namesake charm.

Our server appeared promptly with a complimentary appetizer: pesto popcorn. The pale green kernels crunched with all the amazing flavor of packing material. Whatever herb had coated them had been applied so sparingly it might as well have been a suggestion.

We ordered the same appetizers that I remembered from years ago, curious whether they had maintained their magic that made The Food Market so appealing.

The foot-long mozzarella sticks arrived glistening, their golden crust giving way to molten cheese that stretched like taffy between bites. But after half a pound of breaded dairy, the cheesy richness became a punishment from hell. Each piece sat heavier than the last until I desperately gasped for water like a fish out of water. Swimming to the main entrée, my half-portion of Scallops Deluxe arrived: three Hokkaido scallops plated over a creamy risotto. The menu promised snow peas, but butternut squash appeared on my plate, much to my disappointment.

My friends fared better than I did. The pan-roasted mussels arrived steaming in a white wine broth and were a table favorite. Two other friends ordered the Bone-In Pork and Beans, a thirty-two-dollar plate that prompted one of them to exclaim, "My grandmother would call this highway robbery for a pork chop with cornmeal." Another friend ordered the Red Coconut Curry Duck Breast, remarking that it was good but that the curry flavor was a faint whisper you could barely hear.

> "I think they're trying to please the Hampden crowd. This is curry for people who think black pepper is spicy." — Edgar, whose Thai grandmother taught him to cook

![Steaming mussels in a white wine broth](/assets/images/mussel.png)

Three years ago, The Food Market delivered luxury meals at affordable prices that we students could afford. Today, it delivers an inconsistent experience while charging higher prices, which makes the experience feel disappointing. Some dishes are amazing like the pan-roasted mussels, but others fall flat. When even the complimentary popcorn is disappointing, you wonder whether Chef Gauss has forgotten his roots. The quality of this once-magical place has fallen. A word of caution to those who frequent The Food Market expecting the experience of their memories: the empire might be growing, but the flagship is stumbling.

### Supplementary Information

**The Space**
Tucked along Hampden's "Avenue," The Food Market captures Baltimore's charm. The open kitchen provides theatrical backdrop while waiting for your food to come out. We visited during a Monday night lull, making conversation easy at normal volume levels.

**The Crowd**
An eclectic mix of families and other college students dined with us. The space was quiet enough to hold a conversation at normal volume levels. No music was playing.

**The Bar**
The drink menu had local favorites on tap like Natty Boh along with creative craft cocktails. Impressively, their mocktail menu was also just as long as their normal drink menu, highlighting their careful attention to include all diners.

**The Bill**
The Restaurant Week menu was $55.26 per person. On the à la carte menu, dishes were broken up into "Little" and "Small" plates which ranged from $8 to $24 and "Big" entrees which were $25 to $48. More premium options like certain cuts of steak were available at around $75.

**What We Liked**
Mozzarella sticks; pan-roasted mussels; Bone-In Pork and Beans; Red Coconut Curry Duck Breast. **I would recommend the pan-roasted mussels.**
