<p class="tagline">A meditation on routine, rancidity, and reptilian revelations</p>

It's a noble endeavor to be adventurous. To leave one's comfort and venture into the unknown is a bold thing. Unfortunately for me, I'm a scared chicken, a creature who treats the world like a familiar hamster wheel, running into a groove deeper than the Mariana Trench.

<div class="image-grid">
  <img src="/assets/images/routine-wheel.svg" alt="A hamster wheel representing the cycle of routine">
</div>
<p class="image-caption">The hamster wheel of daily life. Image: author (original SVG).</p>

Around noon each day, I seek that which delights the tongue in nostalgia. Not a sharp, piercing flavor, but one I can blindly recount with a practiced tongue, like a master pianist playing in the dark. Friends have joked they could set a watch by the time I eat lunch.

Except today, something broke my routine.

<hr class="section-divider">

The first bite of food went into my mouth innocently enough, the texture familiar, yielding as my teeth converted it into a gray glob. But then: a wave of rancidity, putridness, and rankness all at once that poured over my taste buds like a tsunami of rottenness. The food was so foul that I just sat there, fork suspended midair like a starstruck idiot. When I finally began processing the taste, I felt like I was sloshing around rusty pennies marinated in three-day-old gym socks inside my mouth.

<div class="pull-quote">
The food was so foul that I just sat there, fork suspended midair like a starstruck idiot.
</div>

I looked around as if something could explain what just happened. I could hear a friend's voice in my head: "Just try something new for once."

Well, now I could say that I tried something new.

What did I just eat? Did I make a mistake preparing my lunch? Impossible. I've shoveled the same food into my mouth a thousand times before. My actions are flawless because they're thoughtless. The fault can't be mine. It's like trying to blame a fish for swimming. And yet here I was, a fish who'd somehow forgotten how to swim.

<hr class="section-divider">

I tried another bite in disbelief, muscle memory overcoming shock. "Why would you do that?" a reasonable person might ask. Well, I crave routine like an addict craves their fix. I gagged, but this time I noticed something underneath the rot: a deep earthiness. The essence of an animal. This flavor awoke my reptilian senses, something ancient I didn't know I had inside of me.

<div class="pull-quote">
This flavor awoke my reptilian senses, something ancient I didn't know I had inside of me.
</div>

I looked down. There it sat: a glistening, red-gray slab of flesh staring back at me with mutual disgust. The surface had an oily sheen, like it'd been fished out of the Gulf of Mexico after the Exxon Valdez spill.

<hr class="section-divider">

My mom called soon after. "How was your day, kanna?"

"Fine," I said, staring at my color-coded meal prep containers in the fridge.

<div class="image-grid">
  <img src="/assets/images/meal-prep.svg" alt="Color-coded meal prep containers representing routine">
</div>
<p class="image-caption">My color-coded life, neatly organized. Image: author (original SVG).</p>

"You sound weird. What's wrong?"

"I just tried something new today."

Pause. "Are you feeling okay? Do you have a fever?"

"No, mom. I just ate something different."

"Different how?" She sounded like I'd announced I was joining a cult.

"Well," she said carefully, as if talking someone off a rooftop ledge, "maybe you should stick to what you know. You like what you like."

<hr class="section-divider">

I didn't finish my food that day. I'm not that changed. But as I scraped the remains into the trash, I understood something had shifted within my psyche. I'd tasted something so uncomfortable that my well-trodden routine suddenly felt less like safety and more like a cage restricting me from new flavors the world had to offer.

I said a silent prayer for this powerful foe that'd bested me in all my 20 years, not out of respect, but out of awe that something could shake my hamster wheel so violently.

<div class="ending-note">
The foe? <strong>Chicken liver.</strong>
</div>
